\begin{abstract}
Game-playing agents assume flat rectangular boards: the convolutional
towers of AlphaZero and KataGo are defined over $\ZZ^2$ tensors and cannot
even ingest a board on a sphere, a torus, or a M\"obius band. We introduce
\emph{Geodesics}, a topological generalization of Go to arbitrary
closed and bordered surfaces (spheres, tori, cylinders, M\"obius bands,
Klein bottles, real projective planes) and lattices in one and three
dimensions, under any of several mesh families --- and ask what
architecture plays it optimally. We present a first implementation of a
\emph{Holonomy-Aware Topological AlphaZero} (HATZ): an AlphaZero-style
self-play agent whose network (i) carries the reflection ($\ZZ_2$) part of
the gauge structure that non-orientable surfaces provably require, via an
orientation cocycle computed from the board's combinatorics and
sheaf-style transport maps conditioned on it; (ii) is exactly equivariant
to each board's automorphism group by construction, replacing the dihedral
data augmentation that has no analogue off the square grid; (iii) pools
through a Morse--Smale multi-persistence hierarchy of the position's
influence field, a canonical topology-grounded coarsening; and (iv) reads
the board as a cell complex, passing messages over vertex--edge--face
incidences with curvature and localized-homology inputs and an ownership
auxiliary head. The network is small ($3.5\times 10^4$ parameters),
dependency-free, and every gradient is hand-derived and numerically
verified. We verify the orientation cocycle against ground-truth
orientability on eleven board types, demonstrate machine-precision
equivariance, and report two self-play pathologies specific to this
setting (transposition-table ko cycles; a degenerate early-pass
equilibrium under Tromp--Taylor scoring) together with their remedies. To
our knowledge this is the first game-playing agent on non-orientable
boards; a full transfer and ablation study is specified and in progress.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The rules of Go never needed a flat grid. Stones occupy vertices of a
graph; liberties are empty neighbors; a chain lives while its liberty set
is non-empty; territory is graph reachability. Everything downstream of
these definitions --- capture, ko, life and death, Tromp--Taylor scoring
--- is intrinsically combinatorial. The $19\times 19$ lattice is a
historical accident of the plane. \emph{Geodesics} takes the rules at
their word and instantiates them on discrete surfaces: geodesic and
Goldberg spheres, square and hexagonal tori, M\"obius bands, Klein
bottles, the real projective plane, cylinders, and cubic lattices in
$E^3$, with vertex, edge, face, or full cell-complex incidence structures.

This generality breaks every assumption baked into modern game networks.
AlphaZero~\cite{silver2018} and KataGo~\cite{wu2019katago} use residual
convolutional towers over rectangular tensors; their $8$-fold dihedral
data augmentation exploits a symmetry group that simply does not exist on
a sphere (whose geodesic boards have icosahedral symmetry) or a Klein
bottle (whose isometries include orientation-reversing translations).
More fundamentally, Weiler et al.~\cite{weiler2021coordinate} prove that
convolution on a non-orientable surface cannot reduce its structure group
to $SO(2)$: any consistent feature transport must be steerable under
reflections, because parallel transport around an orientation-reversing
loop returns frames mirrored. A network that ignores this is
discontinuous across the orientation seam of a M\"obius or Klein board.

We ask: \emph{what is the right architecture for optimal play across
domain geometries and topologies}, and we build a first honest draft of
the answer. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The first game-playing agent on non-orientable and
  arbitrary-genus boards.} We compute a representative orientation
  cocycle $\eps\in Z^1(G;\ZZ_2)$ of the first Stiefel--Whitney class from
  the board's combinatorics alone (dual-BFS over face cycles when faces
  exist; the quotient-gluing rule for fundamental-domain grids), verify it
  against ground truth on eleven board types, and pass messages through
  $\eps$-conditioned transport maps so that crossing the seam applies a
  genuinely different learned map (\S\ref{sec:gauge}).
\item \textbf{Exact automorphism equivariance without augmentation.}
  Weight-shared message passing over the board graph commutes with every
  graph automorphism; we keep all inputs $\Aut$-invariant by construction
  and verify equivariance to machine precision, including under the
  seam-preserving isometries of the M\"obius band
  (\S\ref{sec:equivariance}). This replaces dataset-specific augmentation
  with intrinsic symmetry, with the strict generalization benefit of
  equivariance~\cite{elesedy2021}.
\item \textbf{Morse--Smale multi-persistence pooling.} The ascending and
  descending basin hierarchy of the position's diffused influence
  field~\cite{leventhal2022} segments the board into
  territories-and-frontiers; we use it as a position-dependent,
  parameter-independent pooling operator inside the network --- a
  canonical, topology-grounded coarsening where learned pooling such as
  DiffPool~\cite{ying2018diffpool} only approximates one, and one through
  which backpropagation remains exact (\S\ref{sec:msc}).
\item \textbf{A cell-complex, ownership-supervised value/policy net} with
  curvature (angle-defect) and localized-homology inputs, KataGo-style
  global pooling, and an ownership head; $3.5\times 10^4$ parameters,
  pure-\texttt{numpy} with hand-derived gradients verified numerically to
  $5\times 10^{-6}$ over every parameter (\S\ref{sec:net}).
\item \textbf{Two self-play pathologies and their fixes}: PUCT descents
  that cycle forever through ko transpositions when the in-tree engine
  forgets history, cured by a positional-superko guard; and a degenerate
  early-pass equilibrium under Tromp--Taylor-plus-komi scoring that
  collapses the policy, cured by masking passes early in self-play
  (\S\ref{sec:selfplay}).
\end{enumerate}
The literature intersection we occupy --- game
playing $\times$ gauge equivariance $\times$ non-orientable surfaces ---
is, to our knowledge, empty.

\section{Related work}
\textbf{Game networks and size generality.} KataGo trains one
convolutional net across board sizes with masking and global-pooling bias
layers and extrapolates beyond its training
sizes~\cite{wu2019katago}; Polygames obtains board-size invariance from
fully convolutional trunks with global pooling~\cite{cazenave2020};
ScalableAlphaZero replaces the CNN with a GIN and transfers from small to
large Othello boards with an order-of-magnitude training-time
reduction~\cite{benassayag2021}. All operate on rectangular (or at least
fixed-family) boards; none addresses topology.

\textbf{GNN expressivity.} Message passing is bounded by the 1-WL
test~\cite{xu2019gin,morris2019wl}; Go meshes are locally regular, which
is precisely where 1-WL is weakest, motivating multi-aggregator
updates~\cite{corso2020pna}, higher-order structure, and our cell-complex
lifting, which is strictly more expressive than
1-WL~\cite{bodnar2021cwn}.

\textbf{Gauge equivariance.} Gauge-equivariant CNNs on manifolds and
meshes~\cite{cohen2019gauge,dehaan2021gem} achieve equivariance to local
frame choice via parallel transport; Weiler et
al.~\cite{weiler2021coordinate} prove non-orientable surfaces force the
structure group $O(2)$ (reflections included), demonstrating on a
M\"obius toy model. We implement the discrete reflection ($\ZZ_2$) part,
which is the component non-orientability makes mandatory.

\textbf{Topological deep learning.} Simplicial and CW
networks~\cite{bodnar2021cwn}, combinatorial complex
networks~\cite{hajij2022ccnn}, and neural sheaf
diffusion~\cite{bodnar2022sheaf} pass messages over incidence structures
with (in the sheaf case) learnable transport. Our boards \emph{are} cell
complexes, and our transport maps are a transfer-friendly sheaf: globally
shared matrices conditioned on the edge's cocycle value, rather than
per-edge free parameters.

\textbf{Hierarchical pooling and topological segmentation.} Graph
U-Nets~\cite{gao2019unet} and DiffPool~\cite{ying2018diffpool} learn
coarsenings; our pooling is the Morse--Smale multi-persistence hierarchy
of a scalar field on the board, following the hierarchical GNN
construction of~\cite{leventhal2022}, which gives a canonical
segmentation into basins (territories) meeting along separatrices
(contested frontiers).

\section{Setting: Go on a surface}
\label{sec:setting}
A \emph{board} is a finite graph $G=(V,E)$, optionally equipped with a
2-cell structure (face cycles) making it a closed or bordered combinatorial
surface, produced by \texttt{make\_board(surface, mesh, resolution)}. The
surfaces used here, with the smallest instances in our suite:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
board & $|V|$ & $|E|$ & $\chi$ & orientable & seam edges \\
\midrule
sphere (geodesic $f{=}2$) & 42 & 120 & 2 & yes & 0 \\
plane $D^2$ ($5\times5$) & 25 & 40 & 1 & yes & 0 \\
cylinder ($5\times5$) & 25 & 45 & 0 & yes & 0 \\
torus ($5\times5$) & 25 & 50 & 0 & yes & 0 \\
M\"obius ($5\times5$) & 25 & 45 & 0 & no & 5 \\
Klein ($5\times5$) & 25 & 50 & 0 & no & 5 \\
$\RP^2$ ($5\times5$) & 25 & 48 & 1 & no & 8 \\
box $E^3$ ($4^3$) & 64 & 144 & --- & --- & 0 \\
3-torus ($4^3$) & 64 & 192 & --- & --- & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Rules are graph-intrinsic (positional superko; Tromp--Taylor scoring with
komi $7.5$). Quotient boards are fundamental-domain grids with
identifications; e.g.\ the Klein bottle identifies $(x{+}n_x, y)\sim(x,\,
n_y{-}1{-}y)$ (a flip) and $(x, y{+}n_y)\sim(x,y)$; $\RP^2$ flips both
pairs. The browser implementation and the training package construct
adjacency independently and agree edge-for-edge (verified in tests),
so trained agents transfer between them.

\section{Architecture}
\subsection{Reflection gauge structure from combinatorics}
\label{sec:gauge}
A local orientation at a vertex is a $\ZZ_2$ gauge choice; an assignment
$\eps: E\to\{\pm 1\}$ records whether traversing an edge preserves or
reverses it. Gauge transformations act by $\eps'(u,v)=g(u)\,\eps(u,v)\,
g(v)$ for $g:V\to\{\pm1\}$; the \emph{holonomy} of a cycle,
$\prod_{e\in\gamma}\eps(e)$, is gauge-invariant, and the class
$[\eps]\in H^1(G;\ZZ_2)$ restricted to surface cycles represents the
first Stiefel--Whitney class $w_1$: it is trivial iff the surface is
orientable.

We compute a representative in two ways. When face cycles are available,
a BFS over the dual graph assigns each face an orientation; a shared edge
whose two incident faces traverse it incoherently under the assignment is
a \emph{seam} edge ($\eps=-1$). For fundamental-domain grids the flipped
gluing itself is the seam: $x$-wrap edges on M\"obius and Klein boards,
both wrap families on $\RP^2$.

\begin{proposition}
The seam set produced by dual-BFS is a cocycle representing $w_1$; it is
empty for some BFS iff the surface is orientable. On quotient grids, the
flipped-gluing edge set is likewise a representative.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Dual-BFS fixes a gauge on faces along a spanning tree; conflicts can occur
only on non-tree dual edges, and the conflict indicator is exactly the
obstruction cocycle to extending the orientation, whose class is $w_1$.
For quotient grids, cutting along the flipped gluing yields the orientable
fundamental domain, so a loop reverses orientation iff it crosses the
gluing an odd number of times.
\end{proof}
Empirically, the construction recovers ground-truth orientability on all
eleven board types in our suite (including hexagonal-mesh M\"obius bands
and 3D lattices), with seam counts matching the gluing combinatorics
(Table above).

\subsection{$\eps$-conditioned sheaf transport}
Let $h_v\in\mathbb{R}^D$ be vertex features. Messages pass through
restriction maps conditioned on the cocycle,
\begin{equation}
m_v \;=\; \tfrac{1}{|N(v)|}\!\!\sum_{u\in N(v)}\!\big(W_0 +
\eps(u,v)\,W_1\big)\,h_u
\;=\; W_0\,(A h)_v \;+\; W_1\,(A_\eps h)_v,
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is the row-normalized adjacency and $A_\eps$ its
$\eps$-signed counterpart. Crossing the orientation seam therefore
applies a different learned map --- the mechanism by which neural sheaf
diffusion~\cite{bodnar2022sheaf} encodes orientation reversal and
heterophily --- but with $W_0,W_1$ \emph{shared globally} rather than
learned per edge, so the same parameters transfer across boards, and
because $\eps$ enters linearly the layer reduces to two fixed
aggregation matrices and remains trivially differentiable by hand.

\textbf{Honest scope.} This is the discrete reflection part of the $O(2)$
structure non-orientability mandates~\cite{weiler2021coordinate}; the
continuous $SO(2)$ part (anisotropic kernels with rotational transport
over the mesh's rotation system) is future work, and our aggregation is
isotropic. Further, the network consumes $\eps$ in a fixed canonical
gauge; holonomy-derived inputs are gauge-invariant, but the learned
transport is gauge-covariant only up to reparameterization. We therefore
train with \emph{gauge augmentation}: each self-play game and each
optimization step resamples $g$ uniformly and uses
$\eps'(u,v)=g(u)\eps(u,v)g(v)$, teaching the invariance the architecture
does not yet guarantee.

\subsection{Cell ranks and inputs}
\label{sec:net}
The board is read as a complex: vertex features always; edge features
always (seam flag, endpoint color pattern); face features when face
cycles exist. Per layer $\ell$,
\begin{align}
H_V &\leftarrow \mathrm{relu}\big(H_V W_s + (A H_V) W_0 + (A_\eps H_V)
W_1 + (M_{EV} H_E) W_{ev} + \bar h\, W_g + b\big),\\
H_E &\leftarrow \mathrm{relu}\big(H_E W_{es} + (M_{VE} H_V) W_{ve} +
(M_{FE} H_F) W_{fe} + b_e\big),\qquad
H_F \leftarrow \mathrm{relu}(\cdots),
\end{align}
with $M_{\cdot\cdot}$ fixed incidence-mean operators and $\bar h$ the
global mean (KataGo-style pooling bias~\cite{wu2019katago}). Inputs are
$\Aut$-invariant by construction: stone/empty indicators from the
mover's perspective, normalized degree, discrete curvature (the
Gauss--Bonnet angle defect $2\pi-\sum_i \theta_i$, whose total is
$2\pi\chi$), localized $H_0$ data (own/opponent group size and liberty
counts --- the persistent-homology features in their cheapest useful
form), and global scalars $[\,\mathbf{1}[w_1\neq 0],\ \chi/|V|\,]$.
Heads: a per-vertex policy logit plus a pooled pass logit; a pooled
$\tanh$ value; and a per-vertex $\tanh$ \emph{ownership} head supervised
by final Tromp--Taylor territory, KataGo's auxiliary target adapted to
graphs. Total: $34{,}916$ parameters at width $32\times 3$ layers ---
$\sim\!140$\,KB, comfortably client-side. All gradients are hand-derived
in \texttt{numpy}; central-difference verification over every parameter
gives worst relative error $4.5\times10^{-6}$ (M\"obius: seam transport
$+$ pooling $+$ ownership) and $5.8\times10^{-7}$ (tetrahedron: full
vertex--edge--face coupling).

\subsection{Morse--Smale multi-persistence pooling}
\label{sec:msc}
Let $f_0$ be the two-step mean-diffused stone field of the position. Its
ascending and descending basin partitions --- computed by the same
union-find persistence hierarchy that drives our earlier hierarchical
GNN~\cite{leventhal2022} and, incidentally, the game's built-in engine
--- segment the board into influence basins joined at separatrices, a
decomposition that is Go-semantic (territories and contested frontiers)
and respects the surface's critical structure. At a persistence quantile
we form regions $r$, the one-hot assignment $S\in\{0,1\}^{|V|\times R}$,
mean-pool $P$, region adjacency $A_r$, and insert after the first layer
\begin{equation}
Z = \mathrm{relu}\big((P H_V) W_p + (A_r P H_V) W_r\big),\qquad
H_V \leftarrow H_V + (S Z)\,W_u .
\end{equation}
The segmentation depends on the \emph{position}, not on parameters, so
$S,P,A_r$ are constants of each forward pass and backpropagation through
the block is exact --- a guarantee learned pooling lacks.

\subsection{Equivariance}
\label{sec:equivariance}
\begin{proposition}
If every input feature is invariant under $\Aut(G)$ (and, where used,
under automorphisms of the complex and of $\eps$), the network is exactly
$\Aut$-equivariant: for any automorphism $\sigma$,
$\pi(\sigma\cdot s)=\sigma\cdot\pi(s)$ and $v(\sigma\cdot s)=v(s)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each layer is a composition of permutation-equivariant operators
($A,A_\eps,M_{\cdot\cdot},S,P$ conjugate by the permutation; pointwise
nonlinearities and global means commute with it) with shared weights.
\end{proof}
Verified to machine precision: the empty-board policy is constant on the
WL orbits of the geodesic sphere (two orbits: the twelve pentavalent and
thirty hexavalent vertices; spread $2\times10^{-15}$) and the torus
(vertex-transitive; spread $0$), and exactly symmetric under the
seam-preserving M\"obius reflection (spread $0$). On non-orientable
boards the \emph{gauge-dependent} remainder --- seam placement --- is the
component gauge augmentation trains away; we report this rather than
hide it.

\section{Self-play training, and two pathologies}
\label{sec:selfplay}
Training is standard AlphaZero: PUCT ($c{=}1.5$, Dirichlet root noise),
visit-count policy targets with temperature $1$ for the first ten moves,
replay buffer, Adam. Two failures specific to this setting are worth
recording.

\textbf{Transposition ko cycles.} Our search merges states in a
transposition table keyed by (stones, mover, passes) and re-seats a
scratch engine per step, which forgets ko history. Once two ko-mirror
nodes exist with PUCT argmaxes pointing at each other, a descent cycles
$A\to B\to A$ forever --- the process hangs, typically from the second
iteration once the tree is deep enough to contain a ko. The fix is the
search-side analogue of the game's superko rule: any state repeated
within a single descent scores as a draw and terminates (with a depth cap
as backstop). Three hundred simulations through a live ko now complete in
bounded time; the guard is a permanent regression test.

\textbf{The early-pass equilibrium.} Under Tromp--Taylor scoring with
positive komi, an immediate double pass is a \emph{guaranteed White win}.
Shallow search discovers this instantly: White's visits collapse onto
pass, half the replay buffer becomes one-hot pass targets, and the
learned policy passes as both colors --- losing every evaluation game to
a random mover. (Loss decreases throughout; the curve looks healthy while
the policy degenerates.) KataGo guards its self-play against early
passing for the same reason~\cite{wu2019katago}; we mask the pass action
from search targets for the first $|V|/2$ moves, after which games run
full length and evaluation strength reflects play rather than the scoring
artifact.

\section{Preliminary verification and evaluation protocol}
\textbf{What is verified now.} Cocycle-vs-orientability on 11 board
types; full-parameter gradient checks ($4.5\times10^{-6}$,
$5.8\times10^{-7}$); machine-precision equivariance as above;
Gauss--Bonnet consistency of the curvature inputs (tetrahedron defect
$=\pi$ per vertex); browser/Python adjacency parity edge-for-edge on
cylinder, Klein, and $\RP^2$ boards; end-to-end training and serving
(checkpoint $\to$ WebSocket bridge $\to$ browser opponent, including seam
reconstruction on remote boards). Short training runs on the
non-orientable trio (M\"obius, Klein, $\RP^2$; 24 simulations, six
iterations) show healthy optimization after the pass guard
(loss $3.92\!\to\!2.81$; parity with a random opponent within noise) ---
reported as smoke evidence, not results.

\textbf{Protocol (in progress).} (i) \emph{Zero/few-shot transfer}: train
on $k$ topologies, evaluate Elo on held-out ones (e.g.\ train
sphere$+$torus, test Klein), against fixed anchors (random; the
hand-designed Morse--Smale engine; a Stage-1 GNN of matched size).
(ii) \emph{Ablations}: remove in turn the $\eps$-transport ($W_1{=}0$),
the MSC block, the ownership head, curvature inputs, and gauge
augmentation; measure Elo and gauge-sensitivity. (iii) \emph{Ceiling}: on
the plane boards only, compare against KataGo as an upper reference.
(iv) \emph{Symmetry}: measure sample efficiency versus an
augmentation-based baseline, testing the equivariance dividend
of~\cite{elesedy2021} in self-play.

\section{Limitations and future work}
No continuous $SO(2)$ steerability yet (isotropic aggregation over the
rotation system); gauge invariance is learned, not guaranteed --- a
strictly equivariant formulation via the orientation double cover is the
natural next step; persistent-homology inputs are localized proxies
($H_0$ statistics) rather than diagrams, and $H_1$ features for eyes/ko
are future work; automorphism groups are used for verification (WL
orbits) but not yet for target symmetrization; and the empirical study
above is the actual test of the thesis. A browser export of the network
is mechanical --- the Morse--Smale machinery already ships in the
client-side engine --- and would make every trained agent playable with
no installation.

\section{Conclusion}
Go's rules are indifferent to topology; the field's architectures are
not. We built the first agent that plays where the boards get strange ---
carrying exactly the reflection structure that non-orientability forces,
the automorphism symmetry each board actually has, and a topological
pooling hierarchy with guarantees --- small enough to ship in a browser
tab, honest about which invariances are proved, learned, and pending.
